Module 4 · Liquefaction and refrigeration

Precooled and dual-pressure systems

Two ways to beat the plain Linde–Hampson cycle: pre-chill the gas, or compress less of it.

Precooled Linde–Hampson

Yield rises as the gas is compressed at a lower temperature. Precooling does this with a separate closed-cycle refrigerator (commonly R134a, ammonia or CO2) thermally coupled to the liquefier through a three-fluid heat exchanger. The gas being liquefied is the primary fluid; the refrigerant is the secondary fluid. The refrigerant's boiling point at its suction pressure sets the limit: 216.6 K for CO2, 240 K for NH3 and 247 K for R134a at 1 bar.

With r = ṁr/ṁ the ratio of refrigerant to gas flow, the energy balance gives

y = (h1 − h2)/(h1 − hf) + r (ha,r − hd,r)/(h1 − hf)

The first term is the plain Linde–Hampson yield. The second is the extra yield bought by precooling. The work also gains a term for the refrigerant compressor:

W/m = [T1(s1 − s2) − (h1 − h2)] + r (hb,r − ha,r)

When the three-fluid exchanger is perfect, the gas reaches the refrigerant's boiling temperature, and the yield is at its maximum, ymax = (h6 − h3)/(h6 − hf), with both enthalpies at that temperature.

Linde dual-pressure system

Compression work for an ideal isothermal compressor is W = ṁRT ln(P2/P1). It falls if the mass flow falls or the pressure ratio falls. The dual-pressure system exploits the first: only part of the gas (ṁ − ṁi) is compressed to an intermediate pressure. The stream ṁi is then added, and the combined flow goes up to the full high pressure. The system has two compressors, a three-fluid exchanger, two J–T valves and two liquid receivers. With i = ṁi/ṁ the intermediate mass ratio, the lecture gives

y = [(h1 − h3) − i (h1 − h2)] / (h1 − hf)

where state 3 is at the high pressure and state 2 at the intermediate pressure. Yield is slightly lower than in the plain cycle, but the work drops by more. In the lecture's comparison for nitrogen between 1 and 200 atm, an intermediate pressure of 50 atm gives y = 0.051 and W/mf = 3866 J/g, against 6840 J/g for the simple cycle. That lifts the figure of merit from 0.112 to 0.198.
