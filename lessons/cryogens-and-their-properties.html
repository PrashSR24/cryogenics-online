What counts as a cryogen

The course defines cryogenics as the science and technology of temperatures below 123 K, and a cryogen as a fluid whose normal boiling point is below that. Ordinary refrigerants such as R134a (about 247 K) or propane (231 K) boil far above it.

Reading a T–s diagram

Animation: a gas cooled at constant pressure (1 atm) condenses along the flat part of the isobar, then cools further as a liquid.

Vertical lines on a T–s diagram are lines of constant entropy, so an ideal expansion is a vertical drop. The region under the dome holds liquid and vapour together. The lecture gives these critical points: nitrogen 33.9 atm at 126 K, hydrogen 13.15 atm at 33.19 K, and helium-4 2.27 atm at 5.25 K.

What does a watt of heat leak do?

The heat needed to boil one litre is density times latent heat. Pick a liquid and a heat leak to see how quickly it boils away.

One watt boils about 1.4 litres of helium per hour but only about 22 millilitres of nitrogen. That factor of roughly 60 is why helium vessels are wrapped in a liquid nitrogen shield.

Key points
  • A cryogen boils below 123 K; the dome on a T–s diagram is the liquid + vapour region
  • Boil-off depends on density times latent heat: about 2.6 kJ per litre for helium, 161 kJ for nitrogen
  • Helium and hydrogen are the hardest to hold because of their low latent heat per litre
