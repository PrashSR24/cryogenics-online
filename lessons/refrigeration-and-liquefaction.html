Refrigeration and liquefaction

How heat is pumped out at low temperatures.

A refrigerator moves heat from a cold space to a warmer one, and that takes work. The best possible performance is set by the Carnot limit: the minimum work per unit of heat removed is

W / Qc = (Th − Tc) / Tc

Try it below. The colder you go, the more work each watt of cooling costs, and real machines need several times the Carnot minimum.

–Ideal COP

Main methods

  • Joule–Thomson expansion: a gas cools when it expands through a valve, but only below its inversion temperature (helium about 43 K, hydrogen about 200 K, nitrogen about 620 K).
  • Linde and Claude cycles: liquefy gas using heat exchangers, with Claude adding an expansion engine for extra cooling.
  • Brayton and Stirling cryocoolers: compact machines for small cooling loads.
Key points
  • Carnot work grows rapidly as Tc falls
  • Hydrogen and helium must be pre-cooled before Joule–Thomson cooling works
  • Cryocoolers suit small loads, liquefiers suit large ones
